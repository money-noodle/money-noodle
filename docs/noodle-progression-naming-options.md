# Noodle progression naming options

> **Saved for later · 2026-08-22 · decision deferred.** These are naming explorations for the Noodle Land
> evidence progression. No group is selected, no current prototype label is changed by this document, and no
> names are ready for `SPEC.md` or implementation. Before any real-noodle origin copy becomes user-facing,
> verify it against reliable culinary and cultural sources; several noodle histories are old, regional,
> shared, or disputed. Progression order describes learning—not the quality or importance of a cuisine.

## Group 1 — real noodle names only

| Level | Name | Actual noodle and origin |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Somen Sage** | **Sōmen:** extremely thin wheat noodles from Japan, commonly served chilled or in light broth. |
| 2 | **Ultimate Udon** | **Udon:** thick, chewy wheat noodles associated with Japan. |
| 3 | **Signal Soba** | **Soba:** Japanese noodles traditionally made partly or entirely with buckwheat. |
| 4 | **Ramen Researcher** | **Ramen:** Japanese alkaline wheat noodles developed from Chinese-influenced noodle traditions. |
| 5 | **Fideo Fact-Finder** | **Fideo:** thin wheat pasta from the Spanish/Iberian tradition, now especially prominent in Mexican and other Latin cuisines. |
| 6 | **Kalguksu Keeper** | **Kalguksu:** Korean knife-cut wheat noodles, typically served in broth. |
| 7 | **Lamian Learner** | **Lamian:** hand-pulled wheat noodles associated with northern and northwestern China. |
| 8 | **Dao Xiao Doubter** | **Dao xiao mian:** knife-shaved wheat noodles strongly associated with Shanxi, China. |
| 9 | **Null Naengmyeon** | **Naengmyeon:** cold noodles from the Korean peninsula, especially associated with Pyongyang and Hamhung traditions. |
| 10 | **Bayesian Biangbiang** | **Biangbiang:** exceptionally wide, hand-pulled belt noodles associated with Shaanxi, China. |

**Strength:** grounded, educational, and culturally rich.

**Concern:** the product must not imply that a later noodle or its cuisine is better than an earlier one.

## Questions for the later naming pass

1. Is progression one public hierarchy, category-specific sublevels, or public levels plus prestige titles?
2. Should real noodle names teach culinary context, or should the product avoid ranking culturally distinct foods?
3. How much explanation can a level name require before the joke becomes friction?
4. Which names remain warm and trustworthy when shown beside serious evidence and funded-account controls?
5. Do the names still work in compact mobile badges, screen-reader labels, dark/light themes, and reduced-motion mode?
6. Which exact evidence events unlock each level without rewarding P&L, deposits, stake, arming, order count, or trade frequency?
