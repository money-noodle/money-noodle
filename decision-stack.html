Seven layers stand between a price move and a filled order.

Each layer can only remove candidates — never add them. What separates the layers is not where they sit in the file tree but what kind of authority they hold: only one kind can put money at risk.

The desk wakes on a market snapshot roughly every fifteen seconds and walks the same stack every time. A candidate is a contract, a side, and a venue. It enters at the top and either falls out at one of the gates or reaches the wire.

Sorting those gates by what they are allowed to do is more useful than sorting them by subject, because it answers the question that matters when something changes: could this edit have made the desk trade more?

Four kinds of decision

Economic

Judges whether money is expected to be made. The only category that can authorize a trade or enlarge one.

Safety

Fail-closed. Can refuse a candidate or shrink a ticket; can never admit one. Ambiguity resolves to "no".

Operator

Human intent — arming, pausing, the kill switch, side and asset switches. Never resumes on its own.

Diagnostic

Measures and records at decision time. Structurally unable to admit or refuse anything, so it keeps scoring rules the desk has switched off.

The stack

Three things the shape is telling you

Safety gates are one-way valves

Nothing red can cause a trade. Widen a price band or lift a cap and the worst case is more volume through gates that already passed it; the economic layers still had to say yes first. That asymmetry is what makes the categories worth drawing.

The rule layer refuses to know the track

Layers 01–04 take no execution mode as an argument, so a paper-only or live-only entry rule cannot be written there — it would not typecheck. Divergence is confined to layers 05–07, where it means capital and plumbing, not judgment.

Disarmed is not deleted

Several gates compute their verdict, stamp it on the decision, and then decline to act on it. They appear teal here rather than red because that is exactly what they are: measurement that has been deliberately stripped of authority.

What this leaves out

Simplified deliberately, in favour of following the shape. The parts most worth knowing are missing on purpose:

  • The switch and reversal path — when the portfolio is full, layer 05 evaluates selling an incumbent to fund a strictly better candidate, which consumes two order slots rather than one.
  • The drain loop — layers 06 and 07 can run several times in one cycle, and every ceiling is re-read per placement rather than once per cycle.
  • Bounded experiments riding alongside the production route with their own capped authorization, and the candidate evaluation lane, which never touches production.
  • Collection, storage, and compaction beneath all of it, plus the stateless hosted read path that has no write authority at all.
  • Exact thresholds move with policy versions. Treat the numbers here as illustrative of the shape and read the symbol for the current value.
